# Naming notes (not part of the site; do not upload)

**Chosen working name: Second Analyst.** It is still unchecked for domain and trademark, and "analyst" invites comparison with a human analyst, so the site makes no claim that it matches one. The earlier options are kept below.

Nothing below has been checked for domain, trademark or existing use. Search for each candidate yourself, or ask a trademark adviser, before you buy a domain or print anything.

## The working name, "Decision Memo"

Reasons to move on from it:
- It names one output. The brief now covers research, monitoring, document analysis, evidence checks, tracking and modelling, and a memo is only one of them.
- It sounds like a document service, which undersells a configurable set of jobs.
- You said earlier that the matching domain was not available.

Reasons to keep it for now: it is already live, it is plain, and nothing about the site depends on it. The name is set in `assets/brand.js`.

## The name you may acquire from an acquaintance

I do not know what it is, so I have not assessed it. Judge it against these tests, and treat convenience as no reason to say yes:
- **Distinct.** Does a search for it return mostly your use, or a crowd of unrelated ones?
- **Fits where the product is going.** Would it still make sense if the product were about pricing, documents or modelling and not competitive intelligence?
- **Sounds right.** Not a chatbot, not an enterprise consultancy, not forced wordplay.
- **Say-able.** Can you say it on a call and have it spelled correctly?
- **Terms of the deal.** Who owns the name, the domain and any trademark after the transfer? Does the acquaintance keep any role, say or claim on revenue? Is there a clause that conflicts with a role you may hold elsewhere? Get it in writing, and have it read by a lawyer before money moves.

## Candidates by territory

Two-word names tend to be more distinctive than one generic word.

1. Intelligence and insight
   - Plain Signal: what the evidence says, without the noise.
   - Evidence Desk: a place where evidence gets worked on.
2. Repeatable work and workflows
   - Standing Brief: a brief that stands and is run again. It pairs with the Start a Brief button.
   - Second Run: the job you run again.
3. Intelligence with judgement
   - Checked Signal: findings that a person has looked at.
   - Judgement Desk: the human part, named.

## What I would do

Prefer Standing Brief if a search finds it free enough to use. It describes the offer (a job set up once and run again), it is not generic AI language, and it fits the Start a Brief action. Its weakness is the overlap with the button label, so the button could become "Start a Standing Brief" or stay as it is. Reject any option that a quick search shows is already a known company in software, data or research. Evidence Desk is the fallback.

Keep the name, the descriptor ("Repeatable intelligence work, with human judgement") and the headline separate. The descriptor sits in the footer and the page descriptions, and the headline on the home page says what the offer does.

Buy a domain once a name survives a search and a trademark check. The site can stay on Vercel until then.
