Why this approach

There are three ways to get intelligence work done. Each trades off cost, effort, depth and control differently.

Path 1

General-purpose AI tools

PromptCheck the evidenceAssembleRepeat next month

Good for quick research, analysis and drafting. When the same question comes back every month, someone still supplies the context, repeats the steps, checks the evidence and turns the result into something reliable.

Upfront costLow
Effort on each runYours

Path 2

An analyst team or bespoke research

Hire or commissionBriefWait for the work

Real depth, and the right choice for complex or sensitive questions. A smaller company may not have the budget, or the technical time, to build its own AI workflow. A mid-sized one may have both and still hold back over privacy, security and governance when internal data is involved.

Upfront costHigh
Fit for a recurring jobOften more than it needs

Path 3

A configured, repeatable service

Choose the jobsAgree scope and guardrailsWorkflow runsA person reviews

You choose the jobs and agree the scope and the guardrails. A configured workflow does the repeatable part the same way each time, and a person is there at setup, at review points and for decisions with consequences.

Upfront costPriced per job
Your dataHandled as agreed in writing

The evidence usually exists, but it sits in separate places, and the patterns that matter are hidden between them.

Mandhir Dua

I am an independent competitive strategy advisor with a background in travel marketplaces, and I help teams out of analysis paralysis.

The gap I keep coming back to is between what a small company can realistically build and what a larger one is comfortable deploying. Smaller teams have the questions but rarely the budget or the technical time. Mid-sized ones often have both, then stall on privacy and governance. One job, configured with you and run on data handled as you agree, is a way through.

I would not automate the judgement about people: how strong or fragile a relationship is, and what only comes from being in the room. I have no client results to show yet. The next step is a pilot on one real job that measures what changes.
