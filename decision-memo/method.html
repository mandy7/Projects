How a memo gets made

Every example follows the same pattern, and each one states which steps it performs today.

  1. 1Sources and numbersA fixed list of sources, or numbers you supply.
  2. 2RecordEach item gets a date, a source and a status.
  3. 3Written rulesScores, ratings and rankings come from rules shown before any result.
  4. 4ReviewA named person approves, holds or overrules each result.
  5. 5MemoOne page, with a source on every line.

This is a design pattern. The four examples on this site do not all perform every step, and none of them fetches sources for you.

Evidence, inference and gaps

Every item is stored with a date, a source and one of six statuses: disclosed, stated by the company, opinion, inference, not disclosed or scheduled.

A gap is logged as a gap, and nothing fills it in. A conclusion that rests on inference is rated as inference, so it cannot pass for a disclosed fact.

Where rules and models come in

Where a workflow uses a language model to interpret text, the model’s role is stated. Calculations and rule-based scoring use explicit code. That makes the rules inspectable and repeatable. It does not guarantee that the inputs, assumptions or conclusions are correct.

The demo and the four examples on this site do not call a language model when you use them.

Fixed, and set with you

Fixed
The steps, the status labels, a source and a date on every item, gaps logged and never filled, and no release without approval.
Set with you
The topic, the sources, the decisions to track, the rating rules, the memo format, the schedule, who reviews, and where the memo goes.
Changes each run
The pages read, the events, the gaps, the ratings and the memo.
Kept between runs
The ledger, the decision history and the review log. A live engagement keeps these. The examples do not.

How you can check the work

  • Every item carries a date and a source, and a gap is shown as a gap.
  • In a live build, each quote a model extracts is checked against the fetched text, and a failure goes to the reviewer. The examples here fetch nothing, so this is a design requirement that you cannot try on this site.
  • The scoring and rating rules are written on the page, and a reviewer can overrule any result.
  • You can ask for a back-test. Give me a past period where you know what happened, and we count what the workflow missed and what it got wrong.

Limits

  • A record is only as good as what goes into it. The examples do not check an entry against the world.
  • The models are simple on purpose, and each example lists what it leaves out.
  • I make no claim about accuracy or time saved. A trial on your own decision would measure both.
