    What happens when you press run

    The analysis frames the decision, lists its assumptions as ranges, draws 5,000 scenarios, finds where each route stops paying, ranks the assumptions the answer depends on, and writes a one-page memo. The result appears at the top. Each run gives the same answer, because the random draws are seeded.

    The decision

    Assumptions

    Each assumption is a range (low, typical, high), not a single number. None of these comes from a filing or a data source; the last column says what evidence would replace it.

    AssumptionLowTypicalHighStatusEvidence that would replace it

    Scenarios

    Every dot is one scenario: nine assumptions drawn at random from their ranges, then the best use of the budget worked out for that scenario. Horizontal: rupees going to quick commerce. Vertical: rupees going to general trade. What remains goes to the own site.

    Which route gets the largest share of the best allocation

    Where each route stops paying

    The line shows what the next ₹1 of spend brings back in contribution, in the typical scenario; the band covers the middle 80% of scenarios. Once the line drops below ₹1, more spend loses money. The tick marks where that happens.

    Which assumption the answer hinges on

    Each assumption is pushed to the low and high end of its range, one at a time, with the others held at typical. The bar shows how far the best split moves, in points of the budget.

    Review

    The memo

    Every number on this page is computed in your browser from nine illustrative assumptions for a hypothetical company. It is not market data and not a forecast. The method (scenario draws, allocation, tipping points, ranking of assumptions) is what carries over to a real client, with their own data in place of the assumptions.
