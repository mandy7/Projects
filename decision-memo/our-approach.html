A different way to do intelligence work

Most businesses want regular intelligence on their market and rivals, but few have people to produce it.

Three ways to get this done

Option 1

General-purpose AI tools

Good for

Exploring a question, summarising a document, a first draft of an analysis.

Where it falls short

For work that repeats, someone still has to decide what to feed it, check what comes back and put the output together each time. Answers can differ from one run to the next, so this month is hard to compare with last month.

Option 2

Analyst teams and research firms

Good for

Complex questions that need experience and judgement, such as a market entry or an acquisition.

Where it falls short

Keeping that level of attention on routine monitoring is expensive. A full-time analyst, or a firm’s day rate, is hard to justify for a question that comes up monthly.

Option 3

A managed intelligence service

Second Analyst runs the repeatable parts in automated workflows, and an analyst checks every output before it reaches you. You agree scope, sources and rules with us up front, and you can read them at any time. It suits work that repeats and one-off decisions alike.

  • Scope, sources and rules agreed in writing
  • Same steps every run, so months compare
  • Every finding links to its source and date
  • A named analyst is accountable
  • Month to month, with no annual term
  • You make the decision

Why in-house is hard

Teams are small

13 of 21 intelligence teams surveyed had five or fewer people (Forrester, 2026).

Skills are hard to hire

64% of mid-sized UK businesses using AI say limited skills hold back wider use (UK Government, 2025). Median UK full-time pay was £39,039 in April 2025, about $52,000 (ONS). Our highest-priced plan is $19,800 a year.

Decisions wait on the work

57% of executives say slow decisions are costing them opportunities (PwC, 2025).
