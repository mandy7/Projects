What shapes the price

    Two things are kept apart. Workflow execution is a configured, repeatable job with defined sources, scope and outputs and agreed limits on usage. Human involvement is setup and the first check, review of exceptions, regular interpretation and advisory support. A plan that is mostly workflow does not include open-ended research, extensive manual review or bespoke consulting. Those are added on purpose, and priced as such.

    Pricing questions

    What does it cost?

    Work is priced as a one-time setup fee plus a recurring fee for running the workflow, with scope adjustments inside agreed limits and optional human support on top. I quote after the brief, because the cost depends on the sources, the quality of the data, how often it runs, the review you want and any integration. I start with the smallest useful job and build nothing large before the value is clear.

    Can I start with one job?

    Yes, and that is the usual way in. Choose one job, see whether it earns its place, and add others later. The list of jobs grows over time.

    How long does setup take?

    I confirm a timeline after scoping. It depends on how many sources are involved and how clean your data is.

    What error rate should I expect?

    It has not been measured. A pilot on a real job would measure what the workflow misses, what it gets wrong, and how much human review it needs.

    Do you run pilots?

    For selected jobs, a short pilot can show whether the approach works with real inputs. We agree the scope, the dates and the measures in advance. A pilot is offered selectively after the first conversation, and it is not a commitment to a free build.
